# Understanding Carbon Credits: A Comprehensive Guide

Carbon credits are essential tools in the global effort to combat climate change. These certificates allow companies to emit a specific amount of greenhouse gases (GHGs), typically one tonne of CO2 or its equivalent, within regulated limits. As the world moves toward reducing emissions, the number of available carbon credits decreases annually, ensuring a steady reduction in overall emissions.

![Carbon Credits Overview](/assets/resources/news/understanding-carbon-credits/cover.png)

## Key Points About Carbon Credits

### Market-Oriented Mechanism
Carbon credits are part of a market-driven approach designed to reduce GHG emissions. They provide a system of allowances that help control emissions, with each company's permitted emissions declining over time.

### Reduction Over Time
Each carbon credit represents one tonne of CO2 equivalent (tCO2e), the unit used to measure greenhouse gas emissions. The number of credits allocated to companies is reduced every year, typically by 2-3%, to encourage continual emissions reductions.

### Carbon Offsetting
Carbon credits are often used for carbon offsetting, where companies invest in projects that remove CO2 from the atmosphere, balancing out their residual emissions after making reductions.

## How Do Carbon Credits Work?

The carbon credit system operates through a structured process:

1. Initial Emissions Reduction
   - Businesses implement energy efficiency measures
   - Adopt cleaner technologies
   - Optimize operations

2. Offset Remaining Emissions
   - Invest in carbon offset projects
   - Remove CO2 from atmosphere
   - Neutralize environmental impact

3. Future Investment
   - Fund initiatives for carbon-free future
   - Support innovative sustainability projects
   - Drive technological advancement

## The Carbon Credit Market

The carbon credit market operates through two distinct channels:

### Voluntary Market
- Non-governmental process
- Voluntary emissions offsetting
- Projected growth to US$ 700.5 million by 2027
- Flexible participation options

### Compliance Market
- Mandatory Compliance Market
- Government-regulated schemes
- EU Emission Trading Scheme (EU ETS)
- Kyoto Protocol compliance

### Compliance Offsets
- Regulated by government systems
- Clean Development Mechanism (CDM)
- Enables industrialized countries to fund emission reduction projects
- Strict monitoring and verification

### Voluntary Offsets
- Third-party auditor certification
- Recognized environmental standards
- Market-driven quality control
- Independent verification processes

## The Kyoto Protocol and The Paris Agreement

### Kyoto Protocol (1997)
- 192 participating countries
- Legally binding targets
- CDM and Joint Implementation mechanisms
- Regular progress reporting

### Paris Climate Accords (2015)
- 196 participating parties
- Goal: Limit global warming to below 2°C
- Preferably limit to 1.5°C
- Carbon credits as transition tool

## Conclusion

Carbon credits represent a crucial mechanism in the global fight against climate change. By providing a structured approach to emissions reduction and offsetting, they enable companies to:
- Manage their environmental impact
- Support sustainability projects
- Meet international climate goals

As markets evolve and regulations strengthen, carbon credits will continue to play a vital role in the transition toward a carbon-neutral future. Their effectiveness in combining economic incentives with environmental protection makes them an indispensable tool for businesses committed to sustainability.
